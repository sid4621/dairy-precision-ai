estimated breeding value mean 
In the context of the study you are viewing on Performance of Superior Dairy Cattle Sires, Estimated Breeding Value (EBV) is a mathematical estimate of the genetic merit of an animal.

Essentially, it predicts how much of a cow's performance (like milk yield) is due to its DNA rather than its environment (like feed or weather) and how much of that quality it will pass on to its offspring.

How EBV is Calculated in this Study
The researchers used the BLUP (Best Linear Unbiased Prediction) method to determine these values. This is considered the "gold standard" because it separates the "nature" from "nurture" by looking at:

The Animal’s Own Performance: How much milk the cow actually produced.

Pedigree Data: The performance of the sire (father), dam (mother), and other relatives.

Environmental Adjustments: Removing the "noise" caused by things like the Temperature-Humidity Index (THI), which the study notes has a major impact on production.

Why EBV Matters for Sires
As the description mentions, 90-95% of genetic advancement in a herd depends on the breeding value of the bull (sire). Since a single bull can father hundreds of daughters through artificial insemination, choosing a sire with a high EBV is the fastest way for a farmer to increase the total milk yield of the next generation.

Key Sires from the Dataset
The study ranked the top 10 sires based on their EBVs. If a sire like O.S. Elmer-XA or M.Z. Merlin-XA is at the top of the list, it means their daughters consistently performed better than the average, even after accounting for environmental differences.

The Role of Heritability
It is important to note that the study found a low heritability value (0.03±0.02) for milk production.

What this means: Only about 3% of the variation in milk yield was due to genetics.

The takeaway: While selecting high-EBV sires is important for long-term improvement, the "low" value suggests that management practices (nutrition, cooling systems, and healthcare) currently play a much larger role in determining how much milk these specific cows produce.
